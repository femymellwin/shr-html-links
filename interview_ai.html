01 / What the product does

An AI-assisted hiring platform described in the resume for job requisitions, candidate intake, resume parsing, scheduled interviews, adaptive text/voice/video questioning, transcription, competency evaluation and human-reviewed assessment reports.

  • The product targets the high-touch hiring lifecycle: job requirements, candidate management, scheduling, interview conduct and structured evidence for reviewers.

  • The scoring framework covers coding, technical and behavioral dimensions, with interview transcription and a reviewer-visible candidate assessment.

03 / End-to-end business and technical flow

  1. Create role and evaluation rubric. A recruiter defines the job requisition, skills, competency expectations and interview settings.
  2. Register a candidate. Candidate profile, uploaded resume and scheduled session become the context available for the interview.
  3. Prepare grounded questions. The application can use role context and available knowledge to prepare role-relevant questions; exact retrieval and prompt assembly are not independently verified.
  4. Start the session. Candidate joins a text or media-based session. For media modes, audio/video handling and transcription services are involved.
  5. Adapt follow-up questions. An LLM considers the prior question, candidate answer and assessment goals to propose the next prompt.
  6. Capture evidence. The system retains interview answers/transcripts and evaluation evidence, subject to consent and retention settings.
  7. Produce and review assessment. AI-assisted competency signals and a structured report are presented to a human interviewer or hiring decision-maker.

06 / MCP: concept and evidence

Claimed in resume; MCP endpoint/tooling unverified

MCP is a standard way an AI client can ask approved tools/resources for external context. In an interview flow, an MCP-compatible adapter could expose role specifications, interview-question libraries or evaluation criteria. MCP is distinct from RAG: MCP defines the interface for obtaining context, whereas RAG describes grounding generation using retrieved evidence. 07 / Data entities and access controls

Representative business data

  • Job requisitions, skills, rubric and question bank
  • Recruiters, candidates, resumes and role matching
  • Interview schedules, sessions, state and consent status
  • Questions, candidate responses, transcripts and media references
  • Competency scores, supporting evidence and interviewer reports
  • Tenant/user access, audit events and data-retention controls

Privacy, security and governance

  • Tenant and role boundaries matter because candidate profiles and interview evidence contain sensitive personal information.
  • Capture consent before recording or retaining voice/video where legally required.
  • Prevent one candidate’s context from entering another candidate’s prompts or reports.
  • AI assessments require evidence traceability, fairness checks, human review and a challenge/correction path.

09 / How to explain it in an interview

60-second product narrative

Interview AI is an AI-assisted recruitment and virtual-interview platform. The workflow moves from requisition and candidate context into live text or voice/video interviews. LLMs support targeted questions and adaptive follow-ups; RAG can ground those questions in role-specific materials, while MCP can expose approved external knowledge. Candidate evidence and scores are presented for human review.

What makes an interview adaptive?

A new question uses earlier answers, role competencies and the active assessment plan as context rather than replaying one fixed script.

What does RAG retrieve?

The conceptual design retrieves job specs, competency criteria and approved knowledge; exactly how this is indexed or searched is unverified.

What is MCP doing?

It can standardize tool/resource access to knowledge services, but the CV does not identify an audited MCP server or tools.

Can AI make the final hiring decision?

The described platform supports human-assisted evaluation; there is no documented basis for claiming autonomous hiring decisions.
