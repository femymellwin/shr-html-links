Airport map

The halls, the roads and the connecting airports

Three parts of the airport explain the famous terms CI, CD, branches and promotion.

The check-in hall

CI · Continuous Integration · stops 3–6

Every bag is scanned and tagged the moment it arrives. In DevSecOps: every code change is scanned, built and tested automatically. You leave the hall holding a boarding pass — one tested release package.

Gates, flight and landing

CD · Continuous Delivery · stops 7–13

Passport control, boarding, the flight and touchdown. In DevSecOps: the release package passes its approvals and is flown to where users are. A person still says "cleared for take-off".

Automatic e-gates

Continuous Deployment

No officer at the desk — any passenger who passed every check walks straight through. In DevSecOps: every release that passes all checks goes live by itself.

Roads to the terminal · branches

Each traveller takes their own road so nobody blocks anyone else. At the check-in desk they join the main terminal, and the trip gets its flight number.

Connecting airports · promotion

The same passenger with the same sealed suitcase hops through a few airports before the final one. Moving on to the next airport is called promotion.

DestinationProduction · stops 10–16

Doors open for a few passengers first, then everyone.

One suitcase, sealed once. It is never repacked between airports — only the local paperwork (settings) changes. Tools like Argo CD or Flux work like the departures board: put the new flight number on the board and the airport follows it.

The control tower never sleeps.

After you leave the airport, the tower keeps watching the skies. In DevSecOps, the team keeps watching the live app — and every trip makes the next one smoother.
